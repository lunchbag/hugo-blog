> **The idea.** Some of the biggest lifestyle decisions you will make may look like business decisions when you make them. This guide is simply an invitation to notice.

## Before you begin

**Some might call it non-committal. I call it an unreal amount of freedom as the bootstrapped founder of a profitable business.**

Let me explain. A few years ago, after my divorce, my life became a blank canvas. I decided to be more intentional about my lifestyle, and I'm pretty proud of where I ended up.

As business owners, we're told to optimize for the highest revenue possible, or for being the biggest company in the space. But when you own your company 100%, you get to actually prioritize yourself in the equation.

Looking back over seven years of building Lunch Money, a lot of the decisions I'm happiest about have something in common: they've given me more freedom. Not just financial freedom. Freedom over my work, my time, how I structure my life, and even how I define success. Seemingly unrelated choices about ownership, profitability, meetings, hiring, remote work, relationships, housing, and growth have collectively shaped the life I have today.

So I sat down and laid out the four types of freedom I have in my life:

- **Work freedom:** I build my business and I answer to myself.
- **Time freedom:** My time belongs to me and I'm in full control of how it's spent.
- **Lifestyle freedom:** I've structured my life to stay flexible, travel, and take breaks whenever I want.
- **Mental freedom:** I define what success and "enough" means to me.

My point is this: it's worth asking yourself what you're optimizing for while you're busy building your business, and not losing sight of it. You might be at a point where you need to give up some freedom now for more freedom later. Or you might be profitable already but running on autopilot to keep making more money. The whole point of bootstrapping is that you own the whole thing, and your goals for it don't have to be purely financial.

This guide is a reflection exercise, not a rulebook. It breaks down each of my four freedoms, what they actually look like in my life, and the exact decisions I've made that helped me get here. Then it opens up to a broader list of freedoms you might care about instead, with questions to help you find your own core four.

> **Keep this light.** None of these freedoms need to be maximized at all times. Your priorities may change with the season you are in. Sometimes it makes sense to trade a little freedom today for something you value more, or for greater freedom later. The goal is awareness, not perfect optimization.

## The freedoms

**Take mine as a case study, not a prescription.**

My four are marked below and listed first. But there isn't one correct combination. Someone else might value financial security much more than flexibility. Someone might happily give up location freedom to build a home and community they love. Someone might trade time freedom for work they find deeply meaningful.

The point isn't to maximize every kind of freedom. It's to be intentional about the ones that matter the most to you. If my core four don't resonate, the rest of the list covers other freedoms you might optimize for. Open the ones that catch your eye.

{{< freedom name="Work freedom" tagline="I build my business and I answer to myself." mine="true" >}}

For me, work freedom means having control over what I build, how I build it, and what kind of company I want to create.

#### What this looks like in my life

- I own 100% of a profitable business.
- I have no investors, no board, and no outside stakeholders telling me how to run it.
- Profitability means I don't need to fundraise or constantly calculate runway.
- I'm not chasing an exit. I'm building for long-term sustainability.
- I can challenge industry norms, let customers pay what they want, and lock in their price forever.

#### Choices that helped me get here

- Staying bootstrapped.
- Prioritizing profitability.
- Remaining independent.
- Not treating an exit as the ultimate goal.
- Being willing to grow at my own pace.

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}What does work freedom mean to you?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Who do you want to answer to?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What parts of your work do you most want control over?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

{{< freedom name="Time freedom" tagline="My time belongs to me and I'm in full control of how it's spent." mine="true" >}}

Being able to work at 10 p.m. instead of 10 a.m. is schedule flexibility. Time freedom is having hours that don't belong to work at all.

#### What this looks like in my life

- Meetings are limited to two-hour blocks, three days per week.
- I have control over when, where, and how much I work.
- I see my parents every quarter, and every year we take a weeks-long international trip together.
- I can take workations for weeks at a time, exploring somewhere new without traditional vacation time.
- I can take a random weekday off or go for an afternoon hike.
- I no longer drink caffeine, and I usually wake up naturally without an alarm.

#### Choices that helped me get here

- Deliberately limiting recurring meetings.
- Keeping large parts of my calendar unscheduled.
- Building a business that doesn't require me to be online during traditional office hours.
- Becoming comfortable with time that isn't visibly productive.

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}How much of your week currently belongs to you?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What would you do with an extra ten hours every week?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What would your ideal relationship with work and time look like?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

{{< freedom name="Lifestyle freedom" tagline="I've structured my life to stay flexible, travel, and take breaks whenever I want." mine="true" >}}

Flexibility matters enormously to me.

Call it non-committal, but I like being able to change my mind, spend extended periods somewhere else, and make major life decisions without having to unwind a huge number of commitments first.

#### What this looks like in my life

- My company is fully remote, with no office or lease tying me to one place.
- I rent, meaning I have no mortgage anchoring me to a city.
- I'm childfree by choice.
- I can move cities or countries relatively easily.
- I can travel for extended periods without putting my business or life on hold.

#### Choices that helped me get here

- Building remotely.
- Keeping fixed commitments relatively low.
- Renting rather than owning.
- Being deliberate about which long-term responsibilities I want to take on.

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}How much flexibility do you actually want in your life?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What commitments are deeply important to you?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What commitments would you rather avoid?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What would you like to be able to change easily five years from now?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

{{< freedom name="Mental freedom" tagline="I define what success and “enough” means to me." mine="true" >}}

This one took me the longest.

You can own your company and control your calendar while still letting comparison, expectations, and arbitrary milestones control how you feel about your life.

#### What this looks like in my life

- I've let go of a lot of my need for external validation.
- I've let go of toxic or unsupportive relationships.
- I understand that some people are in your life for a season.
- I'm comfortable moving at my own pace, even if that means we're not at $1M ARR yet.
- I'm learning what enough looks like for me instead of constantly moving the goalposts.

#### Choices that helped me get here

- Questioning milestones I had accepted as definitions of success.
- Becoming more comfortable with other people choosing differently.
- Letting relationships change or end.
- Separating bigger from better.
- Paying attention to what improves my life, not only what makes it look more successful.

#### Mental freedom reflection: which ambitions are actually yours?

{{< q >}}Whose definition of success are you using?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What number, title, milestone, or achievement are you currently chasing?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What do you believe will change once you reach it?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Would you still want it if nobody else knew you achieved it?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What does enough look like to you?{{< /q >}}

{{< freedom name="Financial freedom" tagline="Enough financial security that money isn't the primary constraint on your decisions." >}}

#### What this might look like as a founder

- Paying yourself enough that your personal finances aren't constantly dependent on the next company milestone
- Building enough personal savings that a bad quarter doesn't immediately become a personal crisis
- Being able to turn down revenue that comes with bad terms or the wrong customers
- Reaching profitability so the company isn't dependent on the next round of funding
- Building a business that can eventually generate meaningful income or wealth for you
- Having enough financial breathing room to make longer-term decisions

#### Decisions that might support it

- Prioritizing profitability or having a clear path toward it
- Paying yourself rather than perpetually reinvesting everything into growth
- Keeping personal and business burn at levels you're comfortable with
- Building recurring or predictable revenue
- Maintaining personal savings outside the business
- Thinking deliberately about how much of your net worth you want tied to one company

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}What does financial freedom actually mean to me as a founder?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}How much do I need the business to pay me for my personal life to feel financially comfortable?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}How much of my personal wealth am I comfortable having tied up in one company?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Am I building toward a point where money gives me more choices, or simply raising my definition of "enough" as the business grows?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

{{< freedom name="Location freedom" tagline="The ability to live, work, and travel where you want." >}}

#### What this might look like as a founder

- Running your company from another city or country
- Spending a few months somewhere else without disrupting the business
- Taking extended workations
- Hiring the best person regardless of where they live
- Moving closer to family without relocating the company
- Choosing where you live based on your life rather than your company's headquarters

#### Decisions that might support it

- Building remote-first from the beginning
- Hiring a distributed team
- Avoiding an office or physical operation if you don't actually need one
- Building asynchronous communication into the company
- Avoiding becoming the only person who needs to be physically present somewhere
- Considering location requirements before committing to a business model, team structure, or customer base

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}How important is it that I can run my company from anywhere?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Where would I choose to live if the business had no influence on that decision?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Are there decisions I'm making now that could require me to be in a particular place later?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}How much location freedom would I willingly trade for a team, community, customer base, or opportunity that benefits from being together in person?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

{{< freedom name="Creative freedom" tagline="The ability to build what you believe should exist, and pursue the work you find interesting." >}}

#### What this might look like as a founder

- Pursuing products or features because you genuinely believe in them
- Experimenting with ideas that don't have an obvious immediate ROI
- Taking the company in a different direction when your interests change
- Building for customers you care about rather than whichever market is largest
- Saying no to opportunities that would pull the product somewhere you don't want it to go
- Having room for weird ideas

#### Decisions that might support it

- Being thoughtful about whose money you take and what expectations come with it
- Maintaining enough financial runway to experiment
- Avoiding dependence on one customer or partner
- Protecting some product capacity for experimentation
- Choosing a business model that aligns incentives with the kind of product you want to build
- Not optimizing every decision exclusively around short-term revenue

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}How much control do I want over what my company builds and who it builds for?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Which ideas would I pursue if every decision didn't need an obvious short-term ROI?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Are there customers, investors, partners, or other stakeholders whose expectations could meaningfully influence what I'm able to build?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}How much growth or revenue would I be willing to trade for the ability to keep building things I find interesting?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

{{< freedom name="Family freedom" tagline="The ability to build your company while being present for family in the way that matters to you." >}}

#### What this might look like as a founder

- Being able to pick your kids up from school
- Taking meaningful parental leave
- Spending extended time with aging parents
- Living near family even if your customers or team are elsewhere
- Taking family trips without the company grinding to a halt
- Being available when someone you love actually needs you

#### Decisions that might support it

- Building a company that doesn't require the founder to be constantly available
- Hiring people you trust with meaningful responsibility
- Creating flexibility around when and where you work
- Protecting certain family commitments on your calendar
- Building enough financial cushion to step away when necessary
- Considering the demands a particular growth path will place on you before committing to it

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}What role do I want my business to play in the kind of family life I want?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What family moments or responsibilities am I unwilling to consistently sacrifice for the company?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Could I step away from the business for a meaningful period if someone in my family needed me?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}As the company grows, am I creating more capacity for my family or quietly giving more of myself to the business?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

{{< freedom name="Relationship freedom" tagline="The ability to protect your relationships from becoming collateral damage of building a company." >}}

#### What this might look like as a founder

- Having evenings where you're actually present
- Maintaining friendships outside the startup world
- Having enough emotional capacity left for the people you care about
- Being able to say no to networking, conferences, dinners, and other founder obligations
- Not allowing every conversation to become about your company
- Choosing who gets access to your limited time and energy

#### Decisions that might support it

- Setting boundaries around when you're available
- Not treating every networking opportunity as mandatory
- Protecting recurring time for important relationships
- Delegating instead of automatically becoming the bottleneck
- Being deliberate about how much travel and social obligation comes with your role
- Building a culture where being constantly online isn't treated as commitment

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}Which relationships do I want to have enough time and energy to meaningfully invest in?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Does the way I currently run my company leave enough of me for the people I care about?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Which founder obligations do I participate in because I genuinely value them, and which feel obligatory?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Are there boundaries I need to create so the business doesn't automatically get whatever time and energy is left?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

{{< freedom name="Health freedom" tagline="Enough control over how you build your company to take care of yourself while doing it." >}}

#### What this might look like as a founder

- Getting enough sleep even during demanding periods
- Exercising consistently
- Taking a sick day without the company falling apart
- Being able to take a real vacation
- Structuring your work around when you have the most energy
- Building a company you can sustainably run for years

#### Decisions that might support it

- Not making chronic overwork part of the company culture
- Hiring before every responsibility becomes yours
- Protecting time for sleep, exercise, meals, and recovery
- Limiting unnecessary meetings
- Building systems that allow you to step away
- Recognizing when a short-term sprint has quietly become the permanent way you operate

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}Does the way I currently run my business allow me to take care of myself consistently?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Which parts of my health am I unwilling to treat as expendable during busy periods?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}If I continue working the way I do today for the next five years, would I be happy with that?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Is this genuinely a demanding season, or has "just for now" become the permanent way I work?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

{{< freedom name="Security" tagline="The confidence that your company and livelihood aren't constantly one bad month away from crisis." >}}

#### What this might look like as a founder

- Knowing you can make payroll
- Having enough runway to survive a bad quarter
- Not having your entire personal financial life depend on next month's revenue
- Having enough predictability to make longer-term decisions
- Knowing the business can survive losing a customer
- Being able to weather an unexpected setback without immediately going into survival mode

#### Decisions that might support it

- Maintaining an appropriate cash buffer
- Building recurring and diversified revenue
- Avoiding excessive dependence on one customer, platform, or partner
- Keeping fixed costs at a level appropriate for the business
- Being thoughtful about debt and other ongoing obligations
- Paying attention to downside risk, not just upside potential

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}How much uncertainty am I actually comfortable living with as a founder?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}What would need to be true for me to feel secure enough to stop operating from a place of scarcity?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Which risks am I intentionally taking, and which ones have I simply gotten used to?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Would I rather accept slower growth in exchange for more predictability, or does taking more risk feel worth the potential upside?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

{{< freedom name="Community" tagline="The ability to build a company without losing the sense of belonging and connection you want outside of it." >}}

#### What this might look like as a founder

- Having a community that has nothing to do with your startup
- Building long-term friendships with other founders
- Being rooted enough somewhere to develop meaningful local relationships
- Having peers you can ask for help without it feeling transactional
- Contributing to a founder, industry, or local community you genuinely care about
- Not feeling isolated just because you work for yourself

#### Decisions that might support it

- Making time for recurring communities instead of only one-off networking
- Staying connected to people outside your company
- Choosing where you live partly based on the community you want around you
- Joining or creating founder communities that feel genuinely supportive
- Protecting time for relationships that have no business ROI
- Recognizing that extreme location freedom can sometimes come at the expense of deeper roots

#### Questions to think about

{{< q >}}What kind of community do I want around me while I build my company?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Do I want to be deeply rooted somewhere, or does the ability to move freely matter more to me right now?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Have I built relationships outside my company that don't depend on what I can do for someone professionally?{{< /q >}}
{{< q >}}Am I making enough time to belong somewhere, rather than constantly moving on to the next opportunity?{{< /q >}}

{{< /freedom >}}

> **A reminder.** A rooted life can be a free life. A large company can be the right company. A full calendar can hold deeply meaningful work. Freedom is personal, and every version comes with tradeoffs.
